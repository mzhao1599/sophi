# Inspiration for the redesign

Visited in the built-in browser on 2026-10-23, at 1440×900 and scrolled through. Screenshots were taken
for study only and are not in the repository: nothing here copies another site's pictures, words or
logos. Each entry says what the site does well and the one principle this redesign takes from it.

## Framework — https://frame.work/ (configurator: /products/laptop12-diy-intel-series3/configuration/new)

A repairable modular laptop. The configurator is a split screen: a large photograph of the opened
machine stays pinned on the left while the choices scroll on the right, grouped (System, Memory,
Storage, Keyboard), each group saying "Choose an option" until you do. A bar along the top keeps the
running result in view (price, deposit, ship date) next to "Save configuration".

**Taken:** keep the build in view while you choose. On the landing's module list and on the review
screen, the walker drawing stays pinned beside the list, so every tick is answered on the drawing
without scrolling.

## Teenage Engineering — https://teenage.engineering/products/op-1

A synthesiser page that is mostly the object: the device straight on, very large, on a flat warm-grey
ground, and later its own screen graphics enlarged into whole sections. Colour comes from the hardware
itself; the only strong colour is the red record key.

**Taken:** show the product's own interface large, as a picture in its own right. Each step of "How
setup works" carries the app's real screen for that step (the first question, "What I understood", a
module with its reason, the saved configuration), set at full size beside the verb rather than as a
thumbnail.

## WHILL — https://whill.inc/jp/ and https://whill.inc/us/whill-model-c2/

Power chairs. The Japanese home opens on a full-screen clip of the chair being folded beside a café
table, under a line about wanting to go out more: an everyday moment of use, framed by what you want
to do, not by a condition. The US product page explains each feature in the terms of the situation
("tight doorways", a turning radius, three seat widths). Its feature carousel of identical cards is
the part to avoid.

**Taken:** name each module by the moment it helps with, in large type, and keep the mounting place
and the honest limit beside it. No feature-card carousel.

## Creative Intelligence Company — https://creative.inc/ (found on Land-book, search "hardware")

A children's camera. A tilted, bold, flat pictogram of the camera sits over a full-width photograph of
a lived-in family kitchen, with a large two-line headline around it. The drawing is unmistakably the
product; the photograph is unmistakably real life.

**Taken:** the walker is drawn as one bold, flat figure in the page's ink and set against real
pictures of the world around walking aids. The drawing never pretends to be a photograph.

## Vitsœ 606 — https://www.vitsoe.com/us/606

A shelving system sold as parts. It explains modularity as a short run of verbs (start small, add,
reconfigure) and shows the system as a clean line drawing of uprights and shelves, next to plain,
complete sentences about price and honesty.

**Taken:** explain the walker as a system you change over time, in plain verbs, with the parts named
where they mount. The build sheet reads as a parts list you can change, not as a sales table.

## Playdate — https://play.date/

A handheld console. Whole sections are set on a field of the device's own yellow, each headed with a
short bold name for one part ("The Crank."), with large, friendly body text (about 20px) and an
exploded view of the case beside it.

**Taken:** whole fields of flat colour from the object, one per part of the story, and body text set
large enough to read without leaning in (18–20px on the landing page).

## Daylight — https://daylightcomputer.com/

A paper-like tablet, photographed in warm sunlight on grass with a botanical engraving on its screen.
The old engraving reads as intended because it sits inside a warm, physical scene at full size, not as
a small decorative inset.

**Taken:** the archival walking-aid prints are used large, cropped with intent, on warm paper tones,
each with its caption; never as small decorations scattered around the text.

## What the redesign is not taking

Framework's hairline accordion rules, Teenage Engineering's tiny lowercase type, WHILL's card carousel,
Playdate's emoji-style icons in the menu and Daylight's glass navigation panel. Each one either fails
the readability bar for people with low vision or is a device the house rules ban.
