## What it is

Sophi's onboarding page helps a person choose a walker. The walker assistant is the helper on the walker itself, for every day after that. It runs whenever the walker's Raspberry Pi is on: a system service starts it at boot and restarts it if it stops. It is available whenever she asks, by holding the Talk button or by typing on the page. It is not a microphone that is always listening:

- Listening starts only on a Talk press. There is no wake word (the [cloud agent guide](cloud-agents.md#what-always-on-means-here) requires opt-in and local processing for one).
- The model never speaks unprompted. The Pi says a few fixed lines on its own (it's on, offline or online again, a part stopped responding, it needs pairing again), and those are written in Pi code, not by a model.
- Local alerts (an obstacle near a sensor → a buzz or a short sound) run on the Pi without the network.
- The agent can request a named lift height, but that request only asks the Pi to prepare a Confirm target. The Pi checks its lift configuration and refuses unavailable motion. A person must press Confirm at the walker and continuously hold the screen control before any lift motion. The Pi now recognizes the lift, but the voice Confirm/hold path has not yet completed a physical move. Alert-cue changes also require Confirm.

## How a spoken question travels

1. She holds Talk (a button on the handle, or a Pi GPIO button) and hears a short listening sound. The Pi records at most 15 seconds of 16 kHz mono WAV, then stops at release.
2. The Pi sends the clip to the server over HTTPS (`POST /api/v1/devices/{id}/turns`). The server hosts the cloud side on Render.
3. The server sends the clip to Cartesia's speech-to-text (`ink-whisper`, at most 2.5 s) and gets back her words. If there are none, or Cartesia can't be reached, a fixed line answers ("I didn't catch that…" or "I can't hear you right now…") and no model is asked. The clip is dropped after that call. Nothing stores or logs it.

4. The server sends her words, the walker's configuration, the parts it reports and their health, its fresh sensor readings, any open proposal and the last 10 turns to Anthropic's Claude, to two models at once: Claude Haiku 5.5's answer is used unless it is slow, and then Claude Opus 5.5's ([walker assistant](../../services/cloud-api/README.md#walker-assistant)). A typed question, from the walker's page, skips step 3 and goes the same way. The reply carries the answer and, at most, one suggested cues change. Neither step's time is measured live yet.
5. The server checks the reply. A suggested change that breaks a rule is dropped, and a fixed line replaces the answer. A question about the arms gets a fixed line built from what the walker reports. Readings from a simulated sensor are said to be simulated. If the model fails or takes longer than 8 seconds, a fixed answer from the walker's status is used, marked "answered by the fixed replies".
6. When it has a Cartesia key, the server sends the reply text to Cartesia for speech (a stock voice, never a cloned one) and returns the audio with the answer. The server's whole part of a turn stays within 8 seconds (the model at most 8, the voice at most 3, each only what's left), which leaves time for the upload and the audio's download before the Pi gives up at 10. The Pi plays it, or speaks the reply with its own local text-to-speech when there's no audio. The page shows both sides of the conversation.

**Agent tool loop.** Claude chooses `get_distance`, `get_location`, `get_weather` or `show_on_screen` through its structured `tool_call` field. No keyword match starts a tool. The cloud computes `get_distance` from the current validated ToF snapshot, including all zones reported by the ESP32 module (the first eight in the spoken summary); missing, invalid and stale zones are unknown. For GPS tools, the Pi reads one fix and calls a reverse geocoder or Open-Meteo over HTTPS. Raw coordinates go to the chosen provider, never to Claude or the cloud turn request. Each result returns under the same `client_turn_id`; the model may ask for another tool or answer. The loop permits eight tool calls before a fixed limit reply. The cloud stores and voices only the final answer; the Pi can voice the initial “I'll check” line. Weather covers current conditions only. Conditional alert-setting suggestions still require Confirm. `show_on_screen` asks the Pi to pull up one card (`weather`, `location`, `sensors`, `message` or `parts`); the model names only the view and the Pi fills the card from its own data. It is offered only on a walker turn whose request carries `screen`, with only the views listed there; the server refuses any other view with a fixed line, a page turn gets "Please use Talk on the walker", and the step line is "Putting that on your screen." Details: [the show-on-screen guide](show-on-screen.md). The cloud side is tested against a fake model only.

**Walking directions (cloud side built 2026-10-24; the Pi's side is in the [navigation guide](navigation.md)).** A walker turn that carries `navigation` (the Pi has a position source, real or simulated) is offered two more tools: `start_route` with `destination`, the place in her words (1 to 80 characters, one line, checked by the contract's `RouteDestinationSchema`; a bad one is retried once and then falls back), and `stop_route`. Their "I'll check" lines are "Let me find that." and "Stopping the directions." The Pi finds the place, plans the route and speaks the guidance; the cloud never sees coordinates. The model reads the route in progress as `walking_directions` in its context (the Pi's summary: names, rounded distances, minutes, `simulated`), so "How far is it?" needs no tool. At most one route tool runs a turn: once `tool_results` holds a route result, a further tool request is not run and gets "I couldn't finish checking all of that right now." A route tool the server didn't offer gets "I can't give walking directions on this walker." on a walker turn and "Please use Talk on the walker for that request." on the page. The system prompt for an offering turn says directions are not the arms (topic `directions`, never `arms`), to pass the place as she said it, to answer distance and time from `walking_directions` and say when it's simulated, never to invent directions, distances or an arrival, that a route is advice and never a promise the way is passable, and, after a `start_route` result, to say in one short sentence that it found the place and is starting (the Pi then speaks the first direction itself). A walker turn without `navigation` keeps "This walker can't navigate"; a page turn is told directions work only through Talk and not to offer them, since the page can't tell whether the walker has a position source. Typed walker turns on Claude use the same prompt, schema and rules. The "clear or safe" check still applies to every final reply.

`set_lift_height` proposes the named `ground` (5 mm) or `up` (105 mm) trial preset. `move_lift` proposes a signed relative displacement of 1–800 whole millimetres, such as “move the lift up 20 millimetres.” An absolute millimetre request asks for clarification because the lift has no measured origin. The cloud sends only intent, never a motor command, speed or hold lease. The Pi computes relative targets from the ESP32's stored position and checks the installed 5–950 mm soft range; the ESP32 checks that range again. The person must press Confirm while continuously holding the screen's lift control. Direct bench moves physically raised the carriage from 5 → 10 → 30 mm, but the Pi-mediated voice path remains unverified. The position is only an estimate: an interrupted move, missed step, coupler slip or manual movement can make it wrong.

`send_message` is a separate terminal tool proposal. Claude receives only approved caregiver IDs and names, and drafts at most 30 characters when the present user explicitly asks to send. The server checks the recipient, both phones' membership and the text; it never sends from the model step. The Pi reads back the name and exact text, then waits until speech ends plus 1.5 seconds for a fresh left Confirm press. It expires the offer after 30 seconds, clears it on Talk/offline/restart, and sends through the paired-device message route with an idempotency ID. The existing Circle phone receives it. This is in-app messaging, not SMS. A sender phone and an approved caregiver phone must already be joined. A lost response has no recoverable draft; ask again. Message content is stored by Circle for up to 24 hours and deleted with either member. No incoming Circle message is sent to the model.

Incoming messages go the other way without the assistant: the walker fetches a caretaker's messages to her phone after each heartbeat and reads each new one aloud once in its own local voice ("Message from Maya. …"), never through Claude or Cartesia; a Confirm press with nothing to confirm reads the newest again. Details and limits: [interfaces](../interfaces.md) ("Her incoming messages, read aloud").

The Pi sends a location description (which may contain a street address) and weather summary to Claude as tool results. They are not raw GPS coordinates. These results are request data, not instructions; the model must not treat a place name or provider text as a command. If a tool fails or lacks a GPS fix, its result says so and the model must not guess. An exhausted tool budget ends with a fixed failure line. A continuation of a spoken turn reaches the model as spoken ("spoke while holding Talk"), not typed: before 2026-10-24 it read as "typed on the walker", and live the model then answered "please use Talk" after a `get_location` result on 3 of 3 runs. Checked live against the server's earlier model on 2026-10-24 (not yet repeated with Claude) with synthetic voice clips and the Pi's tool results faked by the harness (see the [cloud README](../../services/cloud-api/README.md#walker-assistant)): `get_location`, `start_route` and `stop_route` were picked as expected. Not yet checked with real GPS, a real route, or the Pi itself.

When the Pi can't reach the server (2 missed heartbeats, or a turn past 10 seconds), a Talk press gets a local line built from its own configuration, such as "I'm offline right now. Your short-sound alerts still work."

## The shared nouns

Everything below is defined once in [`walker.ts`](../../packages/contracts/src/walker.ts), and both the Pi and the cloud validate against it.

| Noun | What it is | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Device | A paired walker | Paired with a one-time 6-digit code from the page (10 minutes; 5 wrong codes from one address lock that address out for 10 minutes). The Pi gets a 32-byte credential once; the server keeps only its SHA-256. The credential works only on `/api/v1/devices/...`, and the owner's `walker_owner` cookie only on `/api/v1/walkers/...`. An owner can have up to 8 walkers; re-pairing makes a new one, so forget the old one first. A code works once, so the walker wakes the server with `GET /api/v1/health` before claiming it (a free Render server can take a minute to wake), then adopts the configuration the pairing returns. |
| Walker configuration | The walker's own numbered settings | Version 1 is `WALKER_SETUP` (hand controls, obstacle sensors, voice; alerts by vibration and speech), made at pairing. Each confirmed change adds a version and never edits an old one. It is applied when the Pi's manifest reports that version. |
| Speaking pace | How fast Sophi talks in Cartesia's voice | `slower`, `normal` (a new walker's) or `faster`, kept on the server with the walker but outside its configuration, so the Pi never sees it and needs no update. It changes at once, with no proposal or Confirm: from a turn ("Please talk slower", answered with a fixed line already spoken at the new pace) or the page's Slower / Normal / Faster. It moves nothing and changes no alert. The walker's own voice keeps its pace. Built 2026-10-25, stubs only; not yet heard live. |
| Heartbeat | The Pi's report, every 5 s | Carries the capability manifest (each part's health and `simulated`, and `simulator` when the walker program runs on a computer; a simulator can still have a real part plugged in, so each part's own flag is what counts), the sensor snapshot, and the lines the walker said on its own since the last heartbeat (its notices and offline replies, at most the newest 50, each with how long ago it was said), so the page shows everything the walker said, in order. The answer says which configuration version is current and which proposal is open. 15 s without one means offline: then no part shows as available, no zone as measured, and any open proposal expires for good. |
| Assistant turn | One question and its answer | Voice or text from the Pi, with the snapshot it has at that moment; text from the page. A repeated `client_turn_id` returns the stored answer with a fresh status and runs nothing again. A walker turn's answer can carry the reply as speech in Cartesia's voice (16 kHz mono WAV, at most about a minute); without it (no key, the voice service failed, or the reply is too long), the walker speaks the reply with its own voice. `voiced_by` records the voice the server offered. Each turn records who heard it (typed, or Cartesia's transcript, or nothing when there were no words or transcription failed), who answered (the model or the fixed replies) and which voice the server offered to speak it. |
| Proposal | A change the assistant offers | Only `cues_change` exists. One open per walker; a newer one supersedes it; it expires after 60 s, or for good when the walker goes offline. It is confirmed only by its id, from the Confirm button or the page; voice can't confirm. The first answer wins: answering a proposal that isn't open gets `409 proposal_closed` with the proposal as it stands. The walker never judges expiry by its own clock; a Confirm press always asks the server. The summary is written by the server from the change itself. A proposal names the turn that made it, so a walker that cancelled that turn declines it. |
| Status view | What the page shows and the model reads | Parts with health and `simulated`, zones as measured with their age or as unknown (never clear), the open proposal, the last 10 turns and the last 10 lines the walker said on its own. A zone takes its `simulated` flag from the part that reported it; a zone from a part the walker doesn't list reads as unknown. |
| Button map | Talk and Confirm | Talk: hold to talk and release to send; a press while waiting cancels. Confirm: confirms the open proposal, or plays a "nothing to confirm" sound. The same names apply to GPIO buttons, ESP32 hand buttons and the simulator's keyboard. |

**Rules a cues change must pass** (`checkCuesChange`): at least one cue, no repeats, different from now, every cue deliverable by a part the walker has (vibration needs hand controls; sound and speech need voice; a walker has no screen), and a walker with obstacle sensors keeps vibration or sound, so a conversation can't silently turn obstacle alerts off.

**Freshness across two clocks** (`zoneViewReading`, `zoneViews`): the Pi's own timestamps decide whether a reading was still valid when the Pi sent it. The server's clock decides how long ago the server received it (more than 7 s, one heartbeat plus 2 s, is stale). The two clocks are never compared with each other, so a Pi with the wrong time can't make an old reading look fresh.

## Privacy and consent

- Her voice clip goes to Cartesia to be transcribed, her words go to Anthropic's Claude, and the text of Sophi's replies goes to Cartesia to be spoken (a reply can repeat some of her words). The page and a card on the walker say so before anyone speaks. Both keys live only on the server (Render environment variables entered by Max); the Pi and the page never see them. Cartesia's terms (date reference normalized to October 23, 2026; revision date not reverified) let Cartesia use Inputs and Outputs to train its models unless agreed otherwise, offer an opt-out form, and state no retention period; zero data retention is for enterprise plans only. Cartesia now hears the clip itself, so only scripted, fictional lines go to it: in the demo only the presenter speaks to the walker. Anthropic's API data terms haven't been checked here yet. Judges can type on the page instead, and the page says where typed words go.
- Voice clips stay in memory for one turn and are never stored or logged. Turns and the walker's lines are deleted an hour after they happen. "Forget this walker" on the page deletes the device, its turns, configurations and proposals, and revokes its credential.
- Logs carry ids, outcomes and timings, never words or audio.
- The page's live companion (`activity` on `GET /walkers`) shows what the server did on each turn: which turns it's working on now and, per saved turn, its timings, the rule that decided the reply, who answered and which voice was offered. It holds no words, only kinds, codes and milliseconds; it lives in server memory (a restart empties it), keeps at most 10 turns a walker for an hour, goes at once on Forget, and only the walker's owner sees it. The Pi never reads it.

## Accessibility

Voice is never the only path. Every spoken answer also appears on the page, every proposal can be confirmed from the page as well as the button, and the page works by keyboard and screen reader, with large text and reduced motion.

## Build plan

The full design, including the demo beats and what changed after the plan review, is kept by the lead. The pieces are: (1) this contract; (2) the cloud store, pairing, device auth and heartbeat on Render with Postgres; (3) the assistant turn and proposals; (4) the Pi service with a simulator on a Mac; (5) the "Your walker" page; (6) the demo script; (7) ESP32 firmware for the hand controls and obstacle sensors. Update this guide as each one lands.

Piece 7 is written ([firmware/](../../firmware/README.md)): the hand boards report Talk and Confirm as `button.talk` and `button.confirm` and play the four haptic cues, and the obstacle board reports four zones at 12.5 Hz. It is tested on a computer and cross-checked against the serial contract. One hand ESP32 has been flashed; a temporary P32-to-GND contact reported Talk presses and triggered a buzz felt by the owner. The final buttons and obstacle sensing remain unverified on hardware.
